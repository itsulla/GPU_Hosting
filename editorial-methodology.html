How claims become publishable

An independent, open-weight-first field guide for choosing GPU infrastructure, sizing memory, comparing provider economics, and deploying inference safely.

First-party source hierarchy

Model claims come from first-party official model cards and vendor documentation. Hardware claims come from manufacturer specifications; cloud prices come from provider pricing pages. Rack aggregates are never presented as single-GPU figures. Weight-only estimates are separated from runtime and KV-cache allowances.

  1. Official product documentation, model cards, specifications, pricing pages, terms, and account status.
  2. Reproducible calculations with inputs and caveats exposed.
  3. Third-party benchmark or user evidence only when clearly labeled and linked.
  4. Unverified, rumored, or dynamically inaccessible facts are omitted or marked pending.

Evidence labels

  • Official specification/documentation: directly stated by the vendor or project.
  • Current provider listing: a configuration or price as labeled by the provider, not generalized to every variant.
  • Estimate/formula: calculated from disclosed assumptions.
  • Editorial judgment: a recommendation based on fit, risk, maintainability, and total cost.

Refresh policy

Volatile facts carry a checked date. A date means the displayed claim was reviewed then; it is not an inventory or price guarantee. Material corrections should update the record, tests, and public checked date together.

Corrections

Use the public correction route. Evidence can change a conclusion regardless of whether a merchant has an affiliate relationship.
